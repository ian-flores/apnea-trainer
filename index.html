    Train safely›

    Dry static only. Everything here is for breath-holds on land — sitting or lying down. Never do breath-holds in or near water without a trained buddy watching you; shallow-water blackout gives no warning and is fatal alone.

    • Never hold your breath while standing, driving, in a bath, or in water.
    • Breathe up calmly. Don't hyperventilate hard — it hides the urge to breathe and raises blackout risk.
    • Contractions (the diaphragm "kicks") are normal and are your cue that the real training has begun. Dizziness, tingling lips/fingers, or tunnel vision mean stop now.
    • One table per day is plenty. Rest fully between sessions and never train through lightheadedness.
    • Skip breath-hold training if pregnant or if you have heart or lung conditions, without medical clearance.

    This tool is for practice and education, not medical advice.
